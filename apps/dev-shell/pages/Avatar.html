The key alone is already avatar-md and avatar-smooth. Initials or a picture — the box is the same square either way.

Four sizes: avatar-xs, avatar-sm, avatar-md, avatar-lg. The initials are a fraction of the box, so they scale with it instead of coming from a table.

avatar-sharp, avatar-smooth, avatar-rounded. avatar-smooth is what the key alone gives. The picture follows the shape — it inherits the radius rather than being rounded separately.

Each is one variable, so every combination falls out on its own. There is no nine-cell table behind this.

Set --avatar-size and everything follows — the box, the picture, and the initials. This is what the fraction buys you.

Two variables. This is also all the “+3” counter needs — it is an avatar, not a new class.

The default layout. A set of people is a list, so the group is a <ul> — and role="list" is written out because WebKit drops list semantics when list-style: none is set.

Avatars overlap and each gets a ring. Set --avatar-group-ring-color to whatever surface the group sits on. The first avatar paints on top, which is also what makes the pointer land where the eye says it should.

One variable rather than three classes. It is measured from the avatar, so it stays proportional at every size.

The six expand effects come from pu-motion. Choose data-motion-layout="overlay" to leave surrounding content fixed or reflow to move it. The first example overlays; the second reflows. Point at a stack or tab into it.

Point at one avatar and it comes to the front of the stack — even the one furthest back. It keeps its place in the row or column: what changes is the stacking order, not the layout. Two things carry the highlight besides the order — it grows and its ring changes — so it never rests on colour alone. It works on either axis, because coming forward has nothing to do with direction.

row follows the writing direction, so RTL needs nothing from you. The overlap follows the axis it is given.

First on top by default. data-stack-order="last" reverses that order. Both stacks contain more than eight avatars to verify that their z-index is derived rather than enumerated.

The group owns both and writes them onto its children, so a stack is always one uniform square. Mixed sizes inside a group are not supported — the overlap is measured from a single box.

The key sits on the <a> or <button> itself for a user menu. Tab to these — the focus indicator is the house pattern, 3px at 2px offset. Give the control a name; a picture with alt="" does not carry one.

A picture with nothing to fall back to needs no wrapper. The same block serves both markups.
